Ana Podariu

Some Results on A Multigraded Analog of The Lefschetz Properties

Sep 30
Abstract: In this talk we will recall the definitions of the Lefschetz Properties on standard graded Artinian rings, as well as some nice results in this original case. We will then introduce Bruzzo, Gondim, Holanda, and Montoya's 2024 definition of the Toric Lefschetz Properties, an analog of the Lefschetz Properties that applies to G-graded algebras. We'll see what results transfer from an N graded case to an N^d grading, and what damage they sustain along the way.

Noah Walker

Products and Symbolic Powers of Determinantal Ideals

Sep 23
Abstract: Given a polynomial ring in mn variables, we can arrange the variables into an (m x n)-matrix and consider its minors. The ideals generated by the minors of this matrix of variables are called determinantal ideals. In this talk, we will look at products and symbolic powers of these ideals and see how they are connected. We will also introduce some of the combinatorial ideas that are useful for working with and understanding these ideals.

Dorian Kalir

Koszul Algebras and Koszul Duality

Sep 16
Abstract: Koszul duality was originally defined for graded algebras over a field and has seen many different generalizations. This is in part due to the many different ways Koszulness can be understood. In this talk we will examine a perspective guided by the twisted tensor construction which points to a generalization of Koszulness by Briggs, Cameron, Letz, and Pollitz from the field setting to morphisms of local rings.

Isaiah Martinez

The singularity category of a Gorenstein ring

Sep 9
Abstract: Given a Noetherian ring R, Ragnar-Olaf Buchweitz introduced the notion of the singularity category of R (under a different name). Buchweitz showed that when R is Gorenstein, the singularity category can be better understood through maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules and complete resolutions. We will briefly discuss the history of Buchweitz's manuscript, present the main result that brings the singularity category of a Gorenstein ring "down to earth," and (if time permits) discuss the significance and impact of Buchweitz's manuscript.

Zach Nason

Quasi-Gorenstein Morphisms II: Electric Boogaloo

Sep 2
Abstract: On Sunday, I talked about joint work with Andrew Soto Levins and Ryan Watson that characterized quasi-Gorenstein morphisms of DG-rings using the virtually G-small property. In CARS today, I'm going to go over the other half of our work, which deals with exact sequences in noetherian local rings, and how to detect exact sequences by measuring whether certain maps of Koszul complexes are quasi-Gorenstein.

Kesavan Mohana Sundaram

On the annihilators of local cohomology modules

Aug 26
Abstract: In 2011, Lynch conjectured that the Krull dimension of the top local cohomology module supported at an ideal I in a Noetherian local ring R, is same as the dimension of R/T, where T is the I-torsion submodule of R. In 2019, Hochster raised a question that, is the top local cohomology module of a local Noetherian domain with support in a given ideal faithful? In the recent years, some cool progress and counterexamples have been made on these questions. So, why not check them out?
